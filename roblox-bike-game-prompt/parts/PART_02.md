[77] `GetWheelTorque` runs inside every sub-step h of [51]: read processed input (throttle, brake, clutch, shift requests, ride mode), apply `RiderAids` limits via the `TorqueLimit` hook, step the source (motor, engine or rider), step clutch and transmission, then return `DriveTorque_Nm` and `ReflectedInertia_kgm2`. With the clutch locked the engine is slaved to the wheel and reflects J_e × ratio² (Kensei K2-250 in 1st: 0.011 × 25.1² = 6.9 kg m², seven times the wheel); while slipping it integrates alone. Wheel torque = source torque × ratio × `Driveline.Efficiency_x`: chain 0.97 (0.93 dry or worn, [231]-[250]), belt 0.96, shaft 0.93, CVT belt 0.88, hub 1.0. Sprockets scale wheel torque by the ratio change and rev-limited top speed inversely: the Kensei ZR-6S Stunt's 15/56 versus a stock-style 16/43 gives 1.39x torque and its 190 km/h ceiling.

[78] `ElectricDrive` models a permanent-magnet motor in three regions. Below base speed ω_b = PeakPower_W / PeakTorque_Nm torque is constant at the controller limit; from ω_b to `FieldWeakening_x` × ω_b (2.0-3.0) power is constant, T = P_peak / ω; beyond that torque falls as P_peak × ω_fw / ω² and is cut to zero at `MaxSpeed_rpm`. Back-EMF scales base speed with voltage, ω_b,eff = ω_b × V_t / `MotorRef_V` (stock `Pack_V`, kept through [370] pack swaps), so sag costs top end first. Volt Wasp X: 42 Nm motor peak, 6 kW, `Reduction_x` 6.1, 0.31 m rear radius, `FieldWeakening_x` 2.9, `MaxSpeed_rpm` 4,300, so 256 Nm at the wheel, base speed 26 km/h, 75 km/h at 3,900 rpm. Efficiency is computed, never tabled: η = P_shaft / (P_shaft + [84] losses), about 0.90 at cruise and under 0.5 in a full-current crawl.

[79] The controller limits two currents independently. Phase current sets torque: T = Kt × I_phase, with Kt = PeakTorque_Nm / `PhaseCurrent_A` (Wasp X: 42 Nm / 300 A = 0.14 Nm/A). Battery current sets power: I_batt = (T × ω_motor + [84] losses) / V_t, capped at the lower of `BatteryCurrent_A` (Wasp X 100 A, about 6 kW at 60 V) and the pack's `MaxDischarge_A` (Wasp X 120 A). Launch is phase-limited, speed battery-limited. Each sub-step, solve for the largest torque meeting both limits, the [84] derate, the [80] low-voltage limit and the [83] mode scale; expose amps and kW to the HUD. Acceptance test: a full-throttle, full-charge Wasp X launch logs about 300 A phase and about 40 A battery at 5 km/h, then 100 A battery with falling phase current above 30 km/h.

[80] `Battery` is an equivalent-circuit pack. Open-circuit voltage comes from a per-cell table over state of charge (SoC 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0 gives 3.00, 3.45, 3.55, 3.70, 3.95, 4.20 V) times the series count, so `PackFull_V` and `PackEmpty_V` are cells × 4.20 and × 3.00, while `Pack_V` keeps the canon nominal label. Terminal voltage V_t = OCV − I × `Pack_ohm`, with regen current negative. Wasp X: 16S, 60 V nominal, 67.2 V full, 48 V empty, 0.05 ohm, sagging about 5 V at 100 A. The low-voltage limit is emergent, I_max = (OCV − PackEmpty_V) / R, so a nearly empty pack delivers less power with no scripted nerf. Below 10% SoC also scale `BatteryCurrent_A` linearly to 40% at 0% (limp mode). `Capacity_Wh` is canon (Wasp X 2,400 Wh) and fixes the SoC scale.

[81] Energy is integrated, never scripted. Each sub-step the client adds OCV × I × h / 3600 to `EnergyUsed_Wh` (pack I²R loss included; regen credited at 90%) and lowers SoC, so Wh/km emerges from aero, rolling resistance, climbs and losses. The server stays authoritative: from the 10 Hz samples of [75] it integrates road-load energy (inertia, drag, rolling and climb power over efficiency) and writes the `Charge_Wh` attribute at 1 Hz ([41]); the client snaps to it beyond a 3% difference.
